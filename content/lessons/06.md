# Lesson 6 — Pronouns that point

Ruth taught you verbs; Mark teaches you who does them. The independent
pronouns stand alone for emphasis or contrast: ⲁⲛⲟⲕ (I), ⲛⲧⲟⲕ (thou, you),
ⲛⲧⲟϥ (he), ⲛⲧⲟⲥ (she), ⲁⲛⲟⲛ (we), ⲛⲧⲱⲧⲛ (you), ⲛⲧⲟⲟⲩ (they). The plural
you wears the same English as the singular — the dictionary's second gloss
says plural, the verse says which.
Seven words, and every one carries its English in the dictionary's first
gloss — verify each as you click through, because ⲛⲧⲟϥ also carries
"rather, but", and context decides which you are reading.

Demonstratives point instead of naming: ⲡⲁⲓ (this), ⲫⲁⲓ (this), ⲛⲁⲓ
(these). Two spellings for "this" is dialect, not error — ⲡⲁⲓ leans
Sahidic, ⲫⲁⲓ leans Bohairic, and Mark will show you the second.

Read Mark 1:9–12 in the corpus reader and circle every pronoun. Notice how
rarely the emphatic forms appear: most subjects hide inside the verb, and
the standalone pronoun means the writer is pointing. That pointing is the
whole lesson — everything else this week is practice.

Pass the intermediate verb quiz at 70%, drill Mark 70–129, then read Mark
1:9–12 again and feel the difference a week of verbs makes.

A note on pointing: emphatic pronouns are rare precisely because they mean something — the writer stops the sentence to point. When you see one standing alone, ask what it is being contrasted with; the answer is usually in the previous verse.
