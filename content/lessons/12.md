# Lesson 12 — Purpose and result clauses

Saints withdraw and beseech: ⲁⲛⲁⲭⲱⲣⲉⲓ (withdraw, depart) is what Isaac
does from the world, ⲡⲁⲣⲁⲕⲁⲗⲉⲓ (beseech, entreat) what he does toward
heaven. ⲡⲉⲓⲑⲉ (agree, consent) is rarer — persuasion with a legal flavor,
the entry says, usually coupled with wanting. ⲥⲱⲧⲡ (choose) and ⲧⲁⲙⲓⲟ
(make, create) do the choosing and the making; ⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ (reveal, appear)
does the showing.

Purpose clauses ("in order to...") and result clauses ("so that...") hang
these verbs together: find three purpose chains in Isaac 5–8 and label
which verb is the purpose and which is the result. The shape to watch
is ⲉⲃⲟⲗ (out, forth) closing motion verbs — withdrawal completed, not just
begun. ⲛⲁⲩ (look, behold), ⲥⲱⲧⲉⲙ (to hear), ⲓⲛⲓ (to bring) you already own
from Mark; here they work in longer chains.

Pass the advanced verb quiz at 70%, drill Isaac 50–119, then read Isaac
5–8 with arrows for every purpose.

A note on purpose: purpose clauses answer why, and Coptic marks them plainly once you look. Every purpose you label correctly is worth two vocabulary words, because purposes repeat across texts while nouns do not.

Purpose is the most transferable grammar in the course: learn it in Isaac and you will recognize it in Mark, in Ruth, everywhere.
