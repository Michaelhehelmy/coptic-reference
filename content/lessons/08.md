# Lesson 8 — The converter and relative clauses

Paul's nouns are almost all Greek loans, and that is good news: you already
know them. ⲁⲅⲅⲉⲗⲟⲥ (messenger, envoy),
ⲡⲣⲟⲫⲏⲧⲏⲥ (prophet), ⲙⲁⲑⲏⲧⲏⲥ (disciple, follower), ⲡⲓⲥⲧⲓⲥ (trust,
belief), ⲁⲅⲁⲡⲏ (love), ⲥⲟⲫⲓⲁ (wisdom), ⲇⲩⲛⲁⲙⲓⲥ (power), ⲉⲕⲕⲗⲏⲥⲓⲁ
(church, assembly), ⲡⲛⲉⲩⲙⲁ (spirit), ⲥⲱⲙⲁ (body). Ten words, ten entries,
and the dictionary confirms every gloss — in the right company ⲁⲅⲅⲉⲗⲟⲥ is
"angel", and the guard that checks these glosses strips the dictionary's own
parenthetical qualifiers before comparing, so that match counts.

The grammar this week is ⲉⲧⲉ, the converter that turns a sentence into a
description: "the word which Paul wrote" hangs a whole clause off ⲥⲁϫⲓ.
Find three ⲉⲧⲉ clauses in 1 Corinthians 1:4–8 and draw the arrow from each
one back to its noun. The nouns are free; the grammar is the lesson.

Pass the intermediate noun quiz at 70%, drill 1 Corinthians 40–99, then
read 1 Corinthians 1:4–8 with the arrows in.

A note on loans: Greek nouns keep Greek shapes inside Coptic sentences, which makes them the easiest vocabulary you will ever learn. Use them as anchors — find the loan first, then work outward to the Coptic grammar around it.
