# Lesson 19 — Reading alone: a Shenoute pericope

A pericope is a passage that stands on its own — one argument, entered
and left within five verses. Yours is Shenoute 89 to 93: five segments,
every one carrying real English, forming a single question the monk
asks and answers — who is useful, who sees the good deeds, and who was
useful to no one. Read it once without translating, then again against
the English, then a third time with the English covered.

Ten nouns carry the passage, and four of them walked in from Ruth —
proof that the beginner shelf works inside the hardest work in the
project. You have met ⲣⲱⲙⲉ (man, human being), ϩⲱⲃ (message, thing),
ϩⲏⲧ (heart, mind), and ⲣⲁⲛ (name) before; six
are new: ⲛⲟⲩⲧⲉ (god), ϩⲉ (fall), ϣⲁϫⲉ (word, saying), ⲏⲓ (house),
ⲡⲟⲛⲏⲣⲟⲛ (evil one), ⲉⲣⲏⲩ (fellow, companion). Six new, four
review — count them against the list and pass the beginner noun quiz
at 70%, which is deliberately the gentlest gate on the shelf: the
reading, not the quiz, is the work here.

Drill Shenoute 420–659 first, so the passage meets you already known;
then read 89–93 alone. No new grammar this lesson and no new reading
assignment beyond the window — Shenoute's segmentation and his partial
English you learned in lesson 18, and lesson 16's frames still hold the
clauses together. Lesson 20 opens the whole shelf: seven works, every
drill bank, every checkpoint.
