# Lesson 18 — Shenoute arrives: a seventh work

Every window you have read so far had English printed under the Coptic,
because you were reading the works that carry it. Shenoute is the
difference, measured: the Eagerness ships 1,078 segments and twenty-one
carry English — the other 1,057 hold only the source's ellipsis
placeholder. Your window, verses 82 to 85, stands inside the lucky
twenty-one: the English under these four verses is real, and it stays
real for the whole window.

The second difference is the spine of the text. Shenoute's discourses
have no chapter and no verse — the monks who edited them segmented the
discourse by codex page instead. Your window opens on page GL29:1 and
walks its first four segments; the corpus numbers all 1,078 segments
from 0 to 1,077 so the drills and checkpoints can point at them.

The third difference is damage. Verses 84 and 85 carry the edition's
square brackets where the manuscript is broken — read through them;
what the bracketed words say in English is printed underneath whole.
Verse 84 even lets you hear lesson 17's nouns in their new context:
"They are thieves and apostates, those who do these deeds" — the thief
and the apostate you named, now under indictment.

The vocabulary is Shenoute's own ten verbs, a monk's vocabulary of
blame and grace: ⲣϣⲟⲣⲡ (be first, hasten), ϭⲛⲁⲣⲓⲕⲉ (blame, find
fault), ⲣϩⲙⲟⲧ (give grace, favour), ⲕⲱⲙϣ (sneer), ⲛⲟⲉⲓ
(understand, know), ⲁⲡⲁⲧⲁ (mislead, deceive), ϥⲱϭⲉ (leap, move
hastily), ⲕⲁⲙⲁ (allow place, give opportunity), ⲣϩⲟⲩⲟ (have more,
exceed), ⲧⲗⲧⲗ (drip, let drop).

Pass the advanced verb quiz at 70%, drill Shenoute 240–419 — it runs
past verse 357, where lesson 16's before-word stands its second and
last time — then read Shenoute 82–85 straight through. Lesson 19 reads
alone: one Shenoute passage, whole, with no hand-holding.
