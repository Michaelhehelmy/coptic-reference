# Lesson 13 — The martyr speaks: direct discourse

Victor answers power with speech, and the nouns are all courtroom words.
ⲁⲡⲟⲫⲁⲥⲓⲥ (decision, assertion) is what he gives; ⲡⲁⲣⲣⲏⲥⲓⲁ (openly,
publicly) is how he gives it. ⲑⲗⲓⲯⲓⲥ (oppression, affliction, trouble) is
what he suffers for it — three nouns, one arc, and the dictionary confirms
each gloss.

The cast: ⲣⲣⲟ (king), ϫⲟⲉⲓⲥ (lord, master), ⲇⲟⲩⲝ (duke),
ⲥⲧⲣⲁⲧⲏⲗⲁⲧⲏⲥ (commander, general), ⲉⲓⲱⲧ (father),
ⲁⲡⲁ (father, abbot). Separately, ⲉⲓⲱⲧ is "barley": same spelling,
different harvest. Names
take no glosses here: ⲡⲓⲗⲁⲧⲟⲥ is Pilate, and the entry describes the man
at length for those who want it.

Direct discourse in Bohairic opens with a speech verb and runs: find three
ⲡⲉϫⲉ-style openings in Victor 1–4 — no, find three speeches, mark who
speaks to whom, and notice that the quoting verb never moves. (ⲡⲉϫⲉ has no
headword entry, as lesson 3 established; read speeches through ϫⲱ.)

Pass the advanced noun quiz at 70%, drill Victor 0–59, then read Victor
1–4 aloud. Martyrs declaim; so should you.

A note on speeches: direct discourse in martyrdom texts follows a strict formula — speaker, speech verb, speech. Learn the formula once and every trial scene opens the same way, whatever the charges are.
