# Lesson 1 — The alphabet and its sounds

Coptic is written in Greek letters plus six signs taken from Egyptian
writing, and you meet four of them in the first verses of Ruth: ϣ in ϣⲱⲡⲉ
(to become, befall), ϯ in ϯϩⲁⲡ (to judge, give judgment), ϩ in ϩⲙⲟⲟⲥ
(to sit, dwell), ϭ in ϭⲟⲓⲗⲉ (to dwell, stay).

Open Ruth 1:1 in the corpus reader. The first three tokens are ⲁ ⲥ ϣⲱⲡⲉ:
a past marker, a small pronoun meaning "she", and the verb "happen". Three
separate tokens, three separate jobs — Coptic writes its little grammar
words apart from the big content words, and the reader, the quizzes, and the
drills all work from exactly these tokens.

Your twelve words this lesson are the most frequent content words in the
whole corpus. Click each one through to its dictionary entry and say it
aloud: ⲉⲃⲟⲗ (out, forth), ϫⲱ (say, speak, tell), ⲓⲣⲓ (to make, to do),
ⲃⲱⲕ (go, depart), ϣⲱⲡⲓ (to become, befall), ⲛⲟⲩϯ (god), ϯ (give, pay),
ⲣⲏϯ (manner, fashion, likeness), ⲣⲱⲙⲉ (man, human being), ⲙⲟϣⲓ (to walk,
to journey), ⲟⲩⲁⲓ (one), ⲥⲁϫⲓ (word). Every gloss above is the dictionary's
first gloss for that entry — look them up and confirm.

Work the beginner noun quiz until you pass at 70%, then try the Ruth drills
for verses 0–19. Afterwards read Ruth 1:1–5 straight through. You will not
understand every word; you will recognize the shape of every sentence, and
that is the whole goal of lesson 1.
