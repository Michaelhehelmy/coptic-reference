# Lesson 9 — Prepositions of place and agency

Mark moves people around: out, up, down, in, there. ⲉⲃⲟⲗ (out, forth)
you know; add its directions. ⲉϩⲟⲩⲛ (to inside, inward) against ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ
(to above, upward): one points in, one points up, and the dictionary's
pairs confirm both. ⲉⲙⲁⲩ (thither) and ⲙⲙⲁⲩ (there, therein) are the far
pair — "to there" against "at there", motion against position.

Time words do the same work as place words. ⲧⲉⲛⲟⲩ (now) anchors the
sentence you are in; ⲧⲟⲧⲉ (then) anchors the one just past — the entry
hedges it as "specific sense unclear", which is honest, because ⲧⲟⲧⲉ leans
on its verse. ⲛⲁⲙⲉ (truly) and ⲟⲩⲏⲓ (indeed, verily) strengthen whatever
they touch; ⲡⲱⲥ (how) asks about it — the entry adds "why" in the same
breath, and the verse decides which. Strip the dictionary's
parentheticals when you read — "interrogative adverb" describes the word,
it is not part of the meaning.

Pass the intermediate adverb quiz at 70%, drill Mark 130–199, then read
Mark 12–16 with a finger on every direction word.

A note on direction: Coptic pairs every motion with a direction, and the direction word is often the more important half. When a drill blanks one, cover the verb instead and guess from the direction alone — you will be right more often than you expect.
