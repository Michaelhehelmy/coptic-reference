# Lesson 15 — Reading alone: Ruth whole

Fourteen lessons ends where lesson 1 began: ⲁ ⲥ ϣⲱⲡⲉ, "and it happened".
You can now parse every token in that clause — past marker, pronoun, verb
(to become, befall) — and you know it because the dictionary confirmed each
one, not because anyone told you.

Read all 85 verses of Ruth straight through, in order, without stopping to
translate. Your eyes already own the load-bearing words: ⲉⲃⲟⲗ (out,
forth), ϫⲱ (say, speak, tell), ⲓⲣⲓ (to make, to do), ⲛⲟⲩϯ (god), ϯ
(give, pay), ⲣⲱⲙⲉ (man, human being), ⲥⲁϫⲓ (word), ϩⲏⲧ (heart, mind),
ⲁⲡⲁ (father, abbot), ϩⲱⲃ (message, dispatch, mission), ⲉⲙⲓ (knowledge;
to know), ⲥⲟⲛ (brother), ⲙⲟⲩ (death, die), ⲟⲩⲁⲃ (pure, holy),
ϣⲉⲗⲉⲉⲧ (bride, daughter-in-law), ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ (woman, wife), ⲕⲁϩ (earth,
soil). Nineteen words carrying a whole book — that ratio is what the
frequency bands were for.

Then pass the beginner noun checkpoint at 70%. It draws on the same banks
you have been drilling since lesson 2; nothing here is new except the
stamina. After it unlocks, there is no lesson 16 — there is the corpus,
the dictionary, and everything you can now read.

A final note, not a lesson: the course ends because courses must, not because Coptic does. The dictionary holds eleven thousand entries and the corpus six works; fifteen lessons were the on-ramp. Read one verse a day from here and the language keeps you.
