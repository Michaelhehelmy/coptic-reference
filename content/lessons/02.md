# Lesson 2 — Articles and nouns

Every noun you meet this lesson already appeared in lesson 1's word list;
now learn how Coptic dresses them. The definite article comes first and
agrees in gender and number: ⲡ for masculine singular, ⲧ for feminine
singular, ⲛⲉ for plural (you will meet all three before Ruth 1:9).

Read Ruth 1:1–2 slowly and circle every ⲡ and ⲧ. Then find ⲡ ⲕⲁϩ, "the
land": article ⲡ plus ⲕⲁϩ (earth, soil). Two tokens, and the dictionary
confirms both: ⲕⲁϩ carries "earth, soil; earth, land, country". A few
verses later you meet ⲧⲉϥ ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ, "his wife": the possessive ⲧⲉϥ does the
work English does with word order, and ⲥϩⲓⲙⲉ carries "woman; wife". (ⲧⲉϥ
itself has no headword entry — it is declared in this lesson's
unlisted_forms — so read possessives through the nouns they mark.)

Your twelve nouns: ⲛⲟⲩϯ (god), ⲣⲱⲙⲉ (man, human being), ⲥⲁϫⲓ (word),
ⲙⲁ (place, dwelling place), ϩⲏⲧ (heart, mind), ⲙⲏϣ (multitude, crowd),
ⲁⲡⲁ (father, abbot), ϩⲱⲃ (message, dispatch, mission), ⲉⲙⲓ
(knowledge; to know), ⲥⲟⲛ (brother), ⲙⲟⲩ (death, die),
ⲭⲣⲓⲥⲧⲟⲥ (Christ, the Anointed one). Every gloss here is the dictionary's,
not mine — verify each one when you click through. Two spellings cover two
entries each, and telling them apart is a real skill: ϩⲱⲃ is also "send",
and ⲙⲟⲩ is also "die".

Pass the beginner noun quiz at 70%, drill Ruth 0–29, then read Ruth 1:6–10.
