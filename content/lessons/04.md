# Lesson 4 — The past that happened

Ruth 1:1 opens ⲁ ⲥ ϣⲱⲡⲉ — past marker, pronoun, verb. That little ⲁ is the
whole past tense: Coptic does not conjugate the verb itself for past time,
it parks a marker in front. Find three more ⲁ-opened clauses before Ruth
1:5 and notice what never changes: the verb keeps its dictionary shape.

Then cross to Mark, where the dialect changes under your feet. Mark 1:1
opens ⲧ ⲁⲣⲭⲏ — "the beginning", article ⲧ plus ⲁⲣⲭⲏ, the word every gospel
starts with. Same alphabet, same articles, different spelling habits: this
is Bohairic, and from here on you read both dialects side by side.

Your ten verbs of happening and moving: ϣⲱⲡⲓ (to become, befall), ⲃⲱⲕ
(go, depart), ⲉⲓ (come, go), ⲟⲩⲱⲙ (eat, bite), ⲙⲟϣⲓ (to walk, to journey),
ϣⲉ (go — and, separately, "pig": same spelling, check both entries before
you laugh), ⲉⲓⲣⲉ (act; become, befall; do), ⲟⲩⲁⲃ (pure, holy — a stative,
a state rather than an act), ⲥⲱϣⲉ (drag, creep), ⲙⲟⲟϣⲉ (walk, go).

Pass the beginner verb quiz at 70%, drill Mark 0–29, then read Mark 1:1–4.
