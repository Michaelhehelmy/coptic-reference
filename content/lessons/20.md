# Lesson 20 — Reading ahead: the whole shelf

The last vocabulary sits on the shelf where you found everything else:
fourteen intermediate verbs, this time the verbs of a reader rather
than a monk — ϣⲓⲛⲉ (seek, ask), ϭⲓⲛⲉ (find), ϩⲱⲗ (fly, go, hurry),
ⲕⲧⲟ (turn, surround), ⲉⲛⲕⲟⲧ (sleep), ϣⲗⲏⲗ (pray), ϩⲁⲣⲉϩ (keep,
guard), ⲣϩⲟⲧⲉ (be afraid), ϫⲡⲟ (beget, bring forth, father), ⲙⲡϣⲁ
(be worthy, worth), ⲟⲩⲱϣⲧ (worship, greet, kiss), ⲟⲩⲱϣⲃ (answer,
respond, converse with), ϣⲟⲩϣⲟⲩ (boast, pride oneself), and ϫⲉⲙϫⲟⲙ
(find strength) — three verbs of seeking and the strength it takes.

Your window is the shelf's front door: the Lausiac History, verses 4 to
7, where Palladius addresses the reader directly — "you now desire to
be taught the truly just words" — four segments, all four carrying
English, a fourth-century bishop introducing Egyptian monks to people
who wanted to read about them. Drill lausiac 50–149 first, pass the
intermediate verb quiz at 70%, then read 4–7 straight through.

Then read ahead. The reader holds seven works: Bohairic Mark and First
Corinthians, the Sahidic Ruth, the Life of Isaac, the Martyrdom of
Victor, the Lausiac History, and Shenoute's Eagerness — every drill
bank behind every lesson you passed, every checkpoint at 70%, every
segment browsable with its glossary at your side. One honesty to carry
with you: Shenoute's English stays partial, exactly as lesson 18
measured it, so the shelf now asks you to do in Coptic what you once
needed English for. That was the point of all twenty lessons.
