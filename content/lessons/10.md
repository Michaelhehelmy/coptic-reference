# Lesson 10 — The parables read whole

No new grammar this week — the parables are the exam for lessons 1–9. The
sower sows ⲥⲡⲉⲣⲙⲁ (seed), the seed falls by the wayside, and everything
you need is vocabulary plus articles. ⲛⲟⲩϫⲉ (throw, cast) is what the sower
does with it; ⲕⲁⲣⲡⲟⲥ (fruit, harvest) is what comes back thirtyfold.
ⲭⲱⲣⲁ (land) is where it lands, ⲓⲁⲣⲟ (river) the landmark every Egyptian
knew, ⲧⲱⲃⲉ (brick, adobe) the material of the houses in every parable
about building.

The verbs are ones you own: ⲥⲱⲧⲉⲙ (to hear), ⲛⲁⲩ (look, behold),
ⲓⲛⲓ (to bring), ⲙⲟϣⲓ (to walk, to journey). The parables command hearing
constantly — "whoever has ears", every time. Read Mark 4 slowly, in Bohairic, without translating — follow the
ⲥⲡⲉⲣⲙⲁ from the hand to the ⲭⲱⲣⲁ to the ⲕⲁⲣⲡⲟⲥ and let the repeated words
carry you. Then read it again and translate one verse in writing.

Pass the intermediate verb quiz at 70%, drill Mark 200–279, then read Mark
4:1–4. Lesson 11 leaves the gospel for the desert fathers, and the
sentences get longer.

A note on reading whole: resist translating sentence by sentence on the first pass. Mark the words you know, circle the ones you half-know, and let the repeated ones — sower, seed, land, fruit — carry the story past the gaps.
