# Lesson 7 — Commands and wishes

Paul's first letter to Corinth opens with blessing and ends with building
up, and the verbs in between are mostly things people do to people. ⲥⲙⲟⲩ
covers "bless, praise, thank" — three English verbs for one Coptic act, and
the entry lists all three, so the dictionary is already teaching you that
translation is choosing. ⲧⲱⲃϩ is "pray, entreat": same shape whether the
praying goes up or the entreating goes sideways.

Your ten verbs of acting on the world: ⲕⲱⲧ (build, form), ⲟⲩⲱⲛϩ (reveal,
be revealed, appear), ⲛⲁϩϯ (to believe), ⲧⲱⲟⲩⲛ (arise), ⲟⲩⲱϣ (wish, desire, love), ⲉⲙⲓ
(knowledge; to know), ϭⲓ (figure, manner), ⲟⲩⲱⲙ (eat, bite). Two spellings
cover two entries each, as in lesson 2: ⲉⲙⲓ is noun and verb under one
spelling, and ϭⲓ is separately "suckle" — check both entries the way you
did for ϣⲉ. Notice ⲟⲩⲱϣ against ⲟⲩⲱⲙ: one vowel apart, "love" against "eat"
— minimal pairs like this are why the drills exist.

Pass the intermediate verb quiz at 70%, drill 1 Corinthians 0–39, then
read 1 Corinthians 1:1–4 and mark every verb of blessing.

A note on the wishlist verbs: commands in Coptic are softer than English imperatives, closer to wishes. When Paul commands, he is usually urging. Read the tone before the grammar, and the grammar will follow.
