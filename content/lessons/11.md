# Lesson 11 — Long sentences in a saint's life

The desert fathers get long titles because they held long offices.
ⲙⲟⲛⲁⲭⲟⲥ (monk), ⲁⲃⲃⲁ (Abba), ⲇⲓⲁⲕⲟⲛⲟⲥ (servant, assistant),
ⲡⲁⲣⲑⲉⲛⲟⲥ (virgin), ⲙⲁⲣⲧⲩⲣⲟⲥ (witness), ⲉⲡⲓⲥⲕⲟⲡⲟⲥ (bishop). The entry
for ⲙⲟⲛⲁⲭⲟⲥ leads with "a single one, a solitary one" — the word meant the
solitude before it meant the life. ⲇⲓⲁⲕⲟⲛⲟⲥ is "deacon" when the serving
is ordained; ⲙⲁⲣⲧⲩⲣⲟⲥ is martyrdom as witness unto death, and the Greek
root never forgot it. The odd one is ϩⲙϩⲁⲗ (servant, slave): same work,
lower station, and the dictionary lists both orders.

Institutions need their own nouns: ⲥⲩⲛⲁⲅⲱⲅⲏ (gathering, multitude,
crowd), ⲕⲁⲛⲱⲛ (rule, principle, instruction). Separately, ⲕⲁⲛⲱⲛ is "rod,
stick": the rule and the rod share a spelling, as they often shared a
room. Names need no glosses from me: ⲡⲉⲧⲣⲟⲥ is "Peter" in English, and
the entry says exactly that about the name.

Read Isaac 1–4 and diagram one long sentence: subject, chained clauses,
and everything hanging off ⲁⲃⲃⲁ. Then pass the advanced noun quiz at 70%
and drill Isaac 0–49.

A note on long sentences: Coptic chains clauses with small linking words where English would start a new sentence. Read for the chain, not the period — find the main verb first, then hang everything else off it.
