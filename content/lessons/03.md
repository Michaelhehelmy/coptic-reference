# Lesson 3 — Saying and doing: core verbs

Ruth runs on two verbs: saying and going. ϫⲱ covers "say, speak, tell" —
open its entry and note the second gloss, "sing": one root, and the context
decides. (You will also meet the conjugated form ⲡⲉϫⲉ in Naomi's speeches;
it has no headword entry of its own, so read it through ϫⲱ.)

Your eleven verbs, all dictionary-verified: ϫⲱ (say, speak, tell), ⲓⲣⲓ (to
make, to do), ⲉⲓⲣⲉ (act; become, befall; do), ϯ (give, pay), ⲃⲱⲕ (go,
depart), ⲉⲓ (come, go), ⲙⲟϣⲓ (to walk, to journey), ⲟⲩⲱⲙ (eat, bite),
ⲛⲁⲩ (look, behold), ⲓⲛⲓ (to bring), ⲥⲱⲧⲉⲙ (to hear). Notice how many cover
both directions of an English pair — ⲉⲓ is "come" and "go", ⲃⲱⲕ is "go" and
"depart". Coptic verbs carve the world differently than English ones, which
is exactly why each links to its entry instead of carrying my gloss.

Pass the beginner verb quiz at 70%, drill Ruth 20–49, then read Ruth
1:11–15 and mark every verb you recognize.

A note on method, since verbs multiply: learn each verb with one example sentence from the corpus reader, not from a list. Lists fade; sentences stick. When the quiz shows you a verb you miss, open its entry before the next round — the five seconds compound.
