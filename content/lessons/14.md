# Lesson 14 — Lausiac brevity: sayings of the elders

An elder speaks once and sits down. ⲁⲡⲁ (father, abbot) said — ⲡⲉϫⲉ
again, read it through ϫⲱ — and the saying that follows is usually one
sentence: ⲛⲏⲥⲧⲉⲓⲁ (fast), ⲡⲣⲟⲥⲉⲩⲭⲏ (prayer), ϩⲩⲡⲟⲙⲟⲛⲏ (patience,
endurance, constancy). Three disciplines, three nouns, no verbs needed.
That verblessness is the grammar of this lesson: nominal sentences state
equivalence — X is Y — with no "is" at all.

The people are ⲡⲣⲉⲥⲃⲩⲧⲉⲣⲟⲥ (elder one) and the verbs are the oldest ones
you know: ⲛⲁⲩ (look, behold), ⲓⲣⲓ (to make, to do), ⲉⲙⲓ (knowledge; to
know), ⲟⲩⲱⲙ (eat, bite), ⲥⲃⲱ (doctrine, teaching). Read Lausiac 1–4 and
count the sentences with no verb. Then pass the advanced verb quiz at 70%,
drill Lausiac 0–49, and notice how much Coptic says without verbs.

A parting exercise in brevity, since the lesson is brevity: take the three
disciplines — ⲛⲏⲥⲧⲉⲓⲁ (fast), ⲡⲣⲟⲥⲉⲩⲭⲏ (prayer), ϩⲩⲡⲟⲙⲟⲛⲏ (patience,
endurance, constancy) — and say each one aloud with ⲁⲡⲁ in front of it, as
the sayings do. The grammar you practiced all course is inside those three
noun phrases: article, noun, and nothing else. Lesson 15 drops the training
wheels entirely — the whole Ruth, beginning to end, with only the
dictionary for company.
