# Lesson 17 — The prepositional phrase: of, to, and whose

Lesson 16 framed the clause and set its circumstance. This lesson fills
the frame: the prepositional phrase, which in Sahidic does the work of
English possession, address, and "whose" all at once.

Two words carry most of it. The preposition ⲙ̄ — English "of, to" —
attaches to a noun phrase and the phrase becomes a possession or a
destination. The connector ⲛⲧⲉ (of) does the same job when the phrase
names a person or a thing. Watch them in the window below — Shenoute
verses 100 to 103, every one carrying English — and watch the order
invert: Coptic stands ⲙ̄ or ⲛⲧⲉ in front of the phrase, where
English tacks its "of" onto the end. Verse 100 keeps one such phrase
whole: "a snare of the devil" is a single unit of Coptic with ⲛⲧⲉ
standing at its heart. The same fronted phrase answers English "whose"
— the possessed noun keeps its article and the preposition stands
before it.

The dative does its work with no preposition at all: the phrase ⲛⲁ ⲛ —
English "to us" — rides directly after the verb, and English has to
spell the relation out word by word. Verse 102 sets the two languages
side by side — the English translation says God gives it "to us", and
the Coptic that carries that meaning is the bare dative ⲛⲁ ⲛ. The
same verse range opens destinations with the phrase ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ ⲉ — English
"down to" — which carries verse 100's subject down to a snare and no
further.

The vocabulary is fourteen nouns, several of them abstract: ⲙⲛⲧⲣⲉ
(witness, testimony), ⲣⲉϥϫⲓⲟⲩⲉ (thief), ⲙⲛⲧϫⲁϫⲉ (enmity), ⲅⲛⲱⲙⲏ
(opinion, doctrine), ⲉⲑⲛⲟⲥ (people), ⲟⲓⲕⲟⲛⲟⲙⲟⲥ (steward,
administrator), ⲣⲉⲙϩⲉ (freeman), ϩⲟϥ (serpent), ⲥⲓⲟⲩ (star),
ⲁⲡⲁⲣⲭⲏ (first-fruit), ⲉⲝⲟⲩⲥⲓⲁ (authority, power, right), ⲙⲁⲥⲧⲓⲅⲝ
(whip, lashing), ⲗⲁⲕϩ (corner, extremity, top), ⲁⲡⲟⲥⲧⲁⲧⲏⲥ (rebel,
apostate).
